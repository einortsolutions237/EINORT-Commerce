# Licence Ledger — Preview Placeholder Photography (D-06 / TMPL-06)

This ledger records, per file, the exact source, author, licence and
commercial-use status of every placeholder photograph in this directory.
It is the evidence a human reviews at the Task 2 `checkpoint:human-verify`
gate before any of these files is committed — see `05.1-04-PLAN.md`.

All six photographs were sourced from **StockSnap.io**, a stock photography
catalog that publishes every photo it hosts under the **Creative Commons
CC0 1.0 Universal (Public Domain Dedication)**. StockSnap's own licence
page (https://stocksnap.io/license, retrieved 2026-09-07) states, verbatim:
*"That's why every single image on StockSnap are governed exclusively by
the generous terms of the Creative Commons CC0 license."* — and further:
*"you can put StockSnap CC0 images to any of these usages without buying
the right to do it, acquiring written permission from the image's creator,
or attributing the work to the image creator."*

CC0 / public-domain dedication is one of the three licence families this
project's sourcing rule (05.1-04-PLAN.md Task 1) explicitly accepts —
alongside the Unsplash Licence and the Pexels Licence — precisely because
none of the three carries an attribution obligation this repo would need
to satisfy at the point these images are rendered (they are baked into a
generated screenshot, which cannot carry a credit line).

## StockSnap's licence, in full

Quoted from https://stocksnap.io/license, "StockSnap's CC0 License"
(retrieved 2026-09-07):

> That's why every single image on StockSnap are governed exclusively by
> the generous terms of the Creative Commons CC0 license.
>
> The Creative Commons license that StockSnap uses is the CC0 license.
> Specifically, that license means you can do any and all of the
> following: Download the image file; Publish, revise, copy, alter, and
> share that image; Use the image (as-is or as you've altered it), in
> both personal and commercial contexts.
>
> Moreover, you can put StockSnap CC0 images to any of these usages
> without buying the right to do it, acquiring written permission from the
> image's creator, or attributing the work to the image creator.
>
> You don't even need to attribute the image to the creator, the way you
> do with other CC or traditional copyright licensing schemes.

The same page's "What Can't You Do With This License?" section names one
restriction: images may not be used to imply an endorsement by the
photographer or by a person depicted in the image. None of the six photos
here depict an identifiable person (Task 1's sourcing rule already
excludes recognisable faces for an independent reason), and none of these
placeholder photographs are used in a way that suggests any photographer's
endorsement of EINORT-Commerce — they are undifferentiated segment filler
inside a screenshot-generation script, never attributed or credited
in-product.

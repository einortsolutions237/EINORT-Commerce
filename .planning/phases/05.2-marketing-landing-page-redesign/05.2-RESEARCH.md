## Summary

This phase is unusual in that its hard part is **content correctness, not code**. Technically it is one statically-rendered React Server Component, zero new dependencies, zero new database access, zero new routes — the current `src/app/page.tsx` is 45 lines and the replacement is a longer version of the same shape. The technical risk is concentrated in six existing source-scanning contract tests that will fail the build if the page reaches for a literal colour, a Tailwind palette utility, the gold accent, `brand-accent`, `data-surface="storefront"`, or (critically for D-02) the 05.1 placeholder photographs. All six are documented below with the exact regexes that catch them.

The content risk is real and larger. `strings.plan.*.features` — the copy already shipped on `/onboarding/plan` and `/dashboard/plan` — advertises **at least six features that do not exist in the codebase**: custom domains, discount codes, bulk import/export, staff accounts, delivery zones with fixed fees, and low-stock alerts. Four of those are explicitly in REQUIREMENTS.md's **v2** or **Out of Scope** lists. A landing page that mines the plan copy for differentiator bullets will propagate fabricated claims straight into the surface D-01 exists to protect. § Verified Feature Ledger below is the allow-list: it was built by reading `src/server/**` and `prisma/schema.prisma`, not by reading marketing copy, and it separates what ships from what is merely named in a plan bullet.

On D-02, research found that **CONTEXT.md's own suggested fallback is structurally forbidden.** `tests/unit/preview-photo-isolation.test.ts` fails the build if the token `preview-assets` or `PREVIEW_PLACEHOLDER_PHOTOS` appears in *any* file under `src/` — the six segment placeholder photos cannot be referenced from a page, and they are not in a served directory (`public/` does not exist in this repo). The recommended approach instead reuses 05.1's own sanctioned D-05 fallback shape: resolve the hero visual from the already-shipped `TEMPLATE_PREVIEWS` manifest, and when the manifest is empty (today), render a **copy-only hero with no visual at all** rather than a wireframe or a stock photo. That makes the page honest and shippable now, automatically upgrades to a real screenshot the moment `05.1-09` runs, and requires no tracked "swap the fake image later" debt.

**Primary recommendation:** Build the page as a single static Server Component reading a new `src/lib/strings/marketing.ts` namespace; write every claim from § Verified Feature Ledger only; ship a copy-led hero today whose image slot is fed by `TEMPLATE_PREVIEWS` and renders nothing when empty; and add one source-scanning contract test that pins both the no-inline-prose rule and a banned-claims list so a future edit cannot quietly re-introduce a v2 feature into the pitch.

## Copywriting & Conversion Findings

### Finding 1 — The hero has ~3 seconds and ~57% of all attention. Spend it on clarity, not atmosphere.

NN/g's eyetracking study (updated 2018, still their standing reference) measured **57% of total page-viewing time above the fold**, **74% within the first two screenfuls (≤2160px)**, and **81% within the first three**. The 2010 comparison figure was 80% above the fold — people scroll more than they used to, but "the pattern of a sharp decrease in attention following the fold remains the same." `[CITED: nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/]` Separately NN/g finds users leave pages in **10–20 seconds** unless a clear value proposition holds them. `[CITED: nngroup.com]`

**Implication for D-04's structure:** hero + the *first* differentiator section are where the decision is made. The single most consequential planning choice is therefore **which of the three differentiators goes first**. Recommendation: **payments first**. It is the one thing a Cameroonian merchant cannot get from Wix, Shopify, or a Facebook page, and it is the claim with the deepest real implementation behind it.

**Confidence:** HIGH (primary eyetracking research, named methodology, dated).

### Finding 2 — Outcome-led headlines beat feature-led headlines; short beats long.

The vendor-side consensus (multiple independent SaaS-design write-ups, 2025–2026) converges on: H1 under ~8 words, outcome-framed rather than feature-framed, with reported lifts of 30–40% in above-the-fold engagement when pages switch framings. `[ASSUMED — the specific percentages come from agency content-marketing blogs with no published methodology and should NOT be quoted to the user as fact. The *direction* (outcome > feature, short > long) is corroborated across every source found and by NN/g's value-proposition guidance, so treat the direction as MEDIUM and the numbers as unverified.]`

Y Combinator's own guidance is the cleanest formulation and carries no measurement claim to be sceptical of: state the company name and what it does in the **simplest possible language**, then apply the *email test* — send two sentences to a smart friend and see whether they can explain it back without asking a clarifying question. "You don't need to sound cool — you need to be clear." `[CITED: ycombinator.com/library — How to Pitch Your Company / YC's essential startup advice]`

**Implication:** EINORT's existing tagline, "Create your online store in minutes," already passes the email test. The redesign should *sharpen* it, not replace it with something more atmospheric. A headline that mentions Cameroon, Mobile Money, or "your own address" scores better on the email test than one that mentions "commerce" or "growth."

### Finding 3 — For a product with no customers yet, **stated constraints are the trust signal.**

The recurring finding across early-stage landing-page literature is that *realistic proof builds trust faster than exaggerated claims*, and that **transparent constraints increase credibility because they signal honesty**. Concrete substitutes for testimonials: showing the actual product (a real screenshot, not an illustration), before/after workflow examples, short specific claims with numbers, and naming what the product does *not* do. `[CITED: thevccorner.com — YC landing page formula; hedrick.io; unicornplatform.com — MEDIUM confidence, multiple independent sources agreeing, no primary study]`

Copyhackers' framing of the same idea, which is the most useful operational version: **vague claims create doubt; specific claims create trust.** "We help businesses grow" tells a reader nothing; a specific, checkable statement tells them exactly what's possible and who it's for. `[CITED: copyhackers.com/2022/07/value-proposition-formula/]`

**Implication — and this is the single most important content recommendation in this document:** D-01 is not a limitation to be worked around. It is the page's competitive advantage, *if it is made explicit*. A line like

> "There is no card gateway and no automatic settlement. Money goes to your own Mobile Money wallet, exactly as it does today — EINORT just makes the order, the amount and the proof line up."

does more for a sceptical Douala merchant than any invented merchant count would, because it is the objection they were already forming, answered before they raised it. **Recommend the UI-SPEC deliberately include one honest-constraint line per differentiator section.** `[ASSUMED — the specific placement is a design judgement; the underlying principle is CITED above.]`

### Finding 4 — One dominant CTA per scroll region; the same CTA every time.

Consistent across every source reviewed: one primary action per section band, repeated verbatim rather than varied. This aligns with a rule already enforced in this codebase — `src/app/page.tsx`'s header comment states *"One primary button per page, never two."* `[VERIFIED: src/app/page.tsx:32-35]`

**Implication:** hero CTA and final CTA must use the **same label**. Use `strings.root.cta` = **"Create my store"**, which is already the label on `/signup`'s submit button — so the merchant clicks the same words twice and never wonders whether they landed somewhere else. Do not introduce "Get started free", "Try EINORT", or a second competing verb.

**One structural warning:** there is **no public template gallery route**. `/onboarding/branding` (which hosts the picker) is behind auth. A "Browse all 50 templates" secondary CTA has nowhere honest to go. Either omit a secondary CTA entirely (recommended, and consistent with the one-primary-button rule) or make it an in-page anchor to the template differentiator section. `[VERIFIED: no public template route exists under src/app/]`

### Finding 5 — Market context that should shape the copy's assumptions, not be quoted on the page.

| Fact | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Cameroon internet users / penetration | 12.6M / **41.9%** | `[CITED: datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-cameroon, published 2025-11-08, data Oct 2025]` |
| Mobile connections | 29.0M (**96.4%** of population); **87.5%** broadband-capable (3G/4G/5G) | `[CITED: DataReportal]` |
| Mobile money users | >12M (2025); MTN MoMo >7M active | `[ASSUMED — secondary press aggregation, not an operator filing]` |
| MTN + Orange combined mobile market share | ~93.8% (MTN 57.0%, Orange 36.8%) | `[ASSUMED — secondary source]` |

**Implication:** the audience reaches this page on a phone, on mobile data, very likely on a mid- or low-end Android. Two consequences the planner must carry into the UI-SPEC: (1) **mobile-first layout is not a nicety** — the desktop composition is the secondary case; (2) **weight discipline** — a hero image, a logo and lucide SVGs is the correct budget; no video, no illustration set, no animation library. The existing 44px touch-target floor (`min-h-11`, already applied on the current root page) applies to every CTA.

**Do not put these statistics on the page.** They are audience context, not EINORT claims, and citing third-party market stats on a marketing page reads as padding — precisely the "social proof theatre" D-01 rules out.

### Finding 6 — The supplied Shopify reference is a structure benchmark, not a content benchmark.

Fetching shopify.com on 2026-09-07 returns a hero of **"Be the next AI all-star"** / *"Dream big and build fast on Shopify. The world's best commerce platform."* / CTA **"Start for free"**, followed by ~10 sections (multi-channel, agentic storefronts, POS, global, case studies, Sidekick AI, developer APIs, checkout conversion, Shopify Capital, and a 3-step "Build fast on Shopify" closer). `[VERIFIED: WebFetch shopify.com, 2026-09-07]`

This does **not** match the user's screenshot ("Start an online store for free" hero with email capture, and a "Powering millions of businesses worldwide" logo strip). Shopify runs regional/experimental variants and rotates its hero frequently; the screenshot is a valid capture of *a* Shopify page, just not the one live today.

**Two things worth borrowing, and one worth refusing:**
- **Borrow:** the *typographic confidence* — one very large heading, one short subline, one button, generous vertical rhythm, and a product visual that occupies real estate rather than decorating a corner.
- **Borrow:** the closing 3-step "Build fast on Shopify" pattern — it maps exactly onto D-04's how-it-works + final CTA.
- **Refuse:** the email-capture-in-hero pattern. Shopify captures an email because it has a mature nurture pipeline. EINORT has `resend` installed but **not wired into any send call anywhere in `src/`** `[VERIFIED: STACK.md + grep]`, no list, no sequence — and an unauthenticated public POST form is a spam and enumeration surface that would need its own rate limiter. The hero CTA must be a plain `<Link href="/signup">`. This is also what D-04 specifies.

**Why (d) is the honest answer, not a dodge:**
1. It ships a page that is **finished at every moment** — there is never a fake asset in the tree, so there is no "swap this later" debt to track and forget.
2. It **auto-upgrades**: `05.1-09` regenerates `preview-manifest.ts` (a committed source file) and uploads to R2. The next deploy renders a real screenshot with zero code change in this phase.
3. It reuses infrastructure that is **already merged** (Wave 1): `templatePreviewPrefixFor` (`src/server/images/r2.ts:157`), `publicUrlFor`, and the `TEMPLATE_PREVIEWS` manifest — all shipped and tested. `[VERIFIED: 05.1-02-SUMMARY.md, git-merged]`

const HERO_TEMPLATE_KEY = "flagship-fashion";

- The exact derivative basename under `templates/{key}/` comes from the `templatePreview` preset in `src/server/images/pipeline.ts:128` — read it, don't guess. `[VERIFIED: r2.ts:151 comment says the basename comes from that preset]`

### Pattern 4 — `next/image` in Next 16: `priority` is deprecated

**What:** the LCP/hero image must not use `priority`.

> "Starting with Next.js 16, the `priority` property has been **deprecated** in favor of the `preload` property in order to make the behavior clear." … "In most cases, you should use `loading="eager"` or `fetchPriority="high"` instead of `preload`."
> `[VERIFIED: node_modules/next/dist/docs/01-app/03-api-reference/02-components/image.md § priority, § preload — the installed 16.3.1 docs]`

Six existing call sites in this repo still use `priority` (`login/page.tsx:30`, `signup/page.tsx:38`, `s/[slug]/sections/hero-section.tsx:144`, `s/[slug]/store-header.tsx:99`, `s/[slug]/p/[productSlug]/add-to-cart.tsx:57`) — they predate the deprecation. **Do not copy them.** Note also that `05.1-06-PLAN.md` already instructs "NEVER `priority`" for picker tiles, so this phase is consistent with an in-flight decision.

**Correct hero usage:**
```tsx
<Image
  src={heroSrc}
  alt=""                      // decorative — the H1 is the accessible content
  width={1280}
  height={800}
  sizes="(min-width: 1024px) 720px, 100vw"
  loading="eager"
  fetchPriority="high"

```
- `sizes` is **required** for a responsive image: without it the browser assumes `100vw` and Next generates only a limited `1x/2x` srcset instead of a full width-descriptor set — a real mobile-data cost for this audience. `[VERIFIED: image.md § sizes]`

## Don't Hand-Roll

| Problem | Don't Build | Use Instead | Why |
|---------|-------------|-------------|-----|
| Copy management | Inline JSX strings | `src/lib/strings/marketing.ts` | Binding rule in CLAUDE.md; the object is intended to become the `en` catalogue whole. Inlined copy is copy a future i18n extraction cannot see. |
| Responsive images | `<img>` + hand-written `srcset` | `next/image` with `sizes` | Automatic WebP, correct srcset widths, and CLS prevention. Hand-rolling costs mobile data on exactly this audience. |

| Colour/spacing values | Hex, oklch, or `slate-600`-style palette classes | Semantic Tailwind tokens (`bg-background`, `text-muted-foreground`, `border-border`, `bg-primary`, `bg-card`, `bg-muted`) | Ban #1 and Ban #2 fail the build. This is the mechanism that prevents incident `260823-gu4` from recurring. |
| Radius/typography scale | Ad-hoc `rounded-[14px]`, arbitrary font sizes | `--radius: 0.75rem` scale (`rounded-lg`/`-xl`/`-2xl`) + `font-heading` (Outfit) / `font-sans` (Plus Jakarta Sans) | The design reference is the locked contract; `--radius-*` derivatives are already declared in `globals.css:66-72`. |
| Touch targets | Default `Button size="lg"` (only `h-9` = 36px) | Explicit `min-h-11` (44px) + `px-6`, exactly as the current root page does | Mobile-first, low-end Android market. `[VERIFIED: button.tsx:27 vs page.tsx:38]` |
| Buttons that are links | `<button onClick={router.push}>` | `<Button render={<Link href="/signup" />} />` | The project's `@base-ui/react` idiom, already used on the current root page. `[VERIFIED: src/app/page.tsx:37]` |
| Hero placeholder imagery | Stock photo, illustration, or a wireframe | Copy-led hero + manifest-fed real screenshot | Pattern 3. The one candidate placeholder set is build-banned from `src/`. |

**Key insight:** almost every "custom solution" temptation on this page is a *design-token* temptation — reaching for a specific colour or radius because the semantic token isn't quite the shade you pictured. That is precisely the failure mode `surface-token-isolation.test.ts` exists to catch, and it already cost this project one full retrofit (`260823-gu4`). The correct move when a token doesn't fit is to raise it in the UI-SPEC and change `globals.css` deliberately — never to write the value into a component.

## Common Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1 — Ban #1: a literal colour value in a component
**What goes wrong:** `npm run test:unit` fails on `tests/unit/surface-token-isolation.test.ts` → "ban 1".
**Regex:** `/#[0-9a-fA-F]{6}(?:[0-9a-fA-F]{2})?\b|\boklch\(|\brgba?\(|\bhsla?\(/` over every `.tsx` under `src/app` and `src/components`, with whole-line comments blanked.
**Why it happens:** marketing pages invite gradients, tinted overlays on hero images, and "just this one" accent shades.
**How to avoid:** every colour comes from a semantic token. If a gradient is wanted, express it in `globals.css` as a token and consume it by name.
**Warning signs:** any `style={{ background: … }}`, any `from-[#…]`, any `bg-[oklch(…)]`.

### Pitfall 2 — Ban #2: a Tailwind palette utility
**Regex:** `/\b(zinc|slate|blue|amber|emerald|red|green|yellow|indigo|gray)-[0-9]{2,3}\b/`
**Why it happens:** the design reference is *described* in slate/blue/gold, so `bg-slate-50` feels like "following the reference". It is the exact opposite — a palette utility ignores `[data-surface="storefront"]` and hardcodes one surface's colour into a shared component.
**Mapping the test's own error message gives:** page field → `bg-background`; panel → `bg-card`; quiet fill → `bg-muted`; hairline → `border-border`; helper text → `text-muted-foreground`; ink fill → `bg-primary`.
**Warning sign:** a `shadcn add` that emitted `zinc-*` (`components.json` still carries a vestigial `"baseColor": "zinc"`). Rewrite the generated utility; never exempt the file.

### Pitfall 3 — The gold-accent budget (the most likely failure on *this* page)
**What goes wrong:** `tests/unit/dashboard-nav.test.ts` → "spends the gold accent exactly twice, and only where the contract says". It scans **all** `.tsx` under `src/app` + `src/components`, requires exactly **one** `variant="gold"` in `app-sidebar.tsx`, and **zero** anywhere except a file matching `/order-state/`.
**Why it happens:** gold is EINORT's brand accent and a landing page is exactly where a designer reaches for it — a "Most Popular" pill, a highlighted stat, an accent underline.
**How to avoid:** no `variant="gold"` on this page, full stop. If the UI-SPEC genuinely wants gold as a *decorative* brand highlight (not a status signal), that is a **deliberate contract change** requiring the test's `REQUIRED`/budget list to be amended in the same commit, with the reasoning recorded — because the whole value of gold in this product is that it means "a human needs to look at this now."
**Open question for the UI-SPEC:** the `--gold-accent` *CSS utility* (e.g. `text-gold-accent`) is only banned under the storefront tree (ban #3), not on the apex. So gold-as-a-hairline is not mechanically blocked. Recommend treating it as blocked by intent anyway, and deciding explicitly. `[ASSUMED — the mechanical scope is verified; whether the *spirit* extends to the marketing page is a decision, not a fact.]`

### Pitfall 4 — Ban #6: `brand-accent`
**Regex:** `/\bbrand-accent\b/` — bare token, any prefix, outside `src/app/s/`.
**Why it happens:** `--brand-accent` is the *merchant's own* storefront colour (D-12), which sounds like "the brand colour" when skimming `globals.css`. It is not EINORT's brand colour. EINORT's primary is `--primary` (brand-600).
**How to avoid:** the string `brand-accent` must not appear in this page's source at all.

### Pitfall 5 — The 05.1 placeholder photographs (D-02's trap)
**What goes wrong:** `tests/unit/preview-photo-isolation.test.ts` fails if `preview-assets` **or** `PREVIEW_PLACEHOLDER_PHOTOS` appears in any `.ts`/`.tsx` under `src/`.
**Why it happens:** CONTEXT.md's own D-02 text suggests these photos as a hero fallback — written before the guard shipped in `05.1-04`.
**How to avoid:** Pattern 3, option (d). Also note these files are in `scripts/preview-assets/`, and **this repo has no `public/` directory**, so there is no serving path even if the guard did not exist.

### Pitfall 6 — Inline prose (softer than it looks, but still a real rule)
**The nuance the planner needs:** the "no inline copy" scan in `tests/unit/dashboard-nav.test.ts` is scoped to **`src/components/app-sidebar.tsx` only** — it is not a repo-wide guard. `[VERIFIED: SIDEBAR_FILE constant, dashboard-nav.test.ts:43]`
**So:** an inlined sentence in `page.tsx` would **not** fail any test today. It would still violate CLAUDE.md's binding rule and the strings module's own stated contract.
**Recommendation:** since MKTG-01's honesty clause needs a mechanical guard anyway (§ Validation Architecture), extend that same new test to cover `src/app/page.tsx`'s prose. That converts a convention into a contract for the surface where honesty matters most, using the existing `stripComments` / `QUOTED_PROSE` / `looksLikeProse` idiom verbatim.

### Pitfall 7 — Deprecated `priority` copied from a neighbouring file
See Pattern 4. Six existing call sites will tempt a copy-paste. Use `loading="eager"` + `fetchPriority="high"`.

### Pitfall 8 — Title template double-branding
`title: "EINORT"` renders `EINORT · EINORT`. Use `title: { absolute: … }`. See Pattern 5.

### Pitfall 9 — Claiming "minutes" without the qualifier that makes it true
ONB-04's requirement is "a live, published storefront **within minutes**" — and it is genuinely true of onboarding. But the merchant still has to photograph and list products. Copy that implies a *stocked* store in minutes overstates. The candidate copy above deliberately says "a live address" and "list your first products" as separate beats.

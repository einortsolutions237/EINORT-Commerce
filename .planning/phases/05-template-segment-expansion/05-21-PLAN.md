## Phase Goal

Merchants outside the fashion segment get their own structurally distinct storefront, and the template library reaches 50 visually distinct variations (10 Starter / 15 Business / 25 Professional) that a stranger would not mistake for one another.

Prove the write paths against a real database and a real session: the tier gate, the draft-only write, the discard revert, the token survival rule, and the onboarding seed.

</objective>

Create `tests/isolation/template-switch.test.ts`, reusing the established real-session harness. Copy `storefront-editor.test.ts`'s header verbatim in spirit — why isolation, how actions are invoked, and the failure-reading instructions.

Cases, with test names containing the substrings `05-VALIDATION.md` filters on (`tier`, `discard`, `accents`):

1. **Tier refusal, not trial-elevated (D-06 / D-12).** Construct a merchant on the **Starter** tier with an **ACTIVE trial**, so `canWrite` is true and `canEditStorefront` is true via D-15. Call `switchTemplate` with a `minTier: "professional"` key. Assert it returns the refusal shape `merchantAction` produces for an `EntitlementError`, and — critically — assert that **nothing was written**: the tenant's `draftTemplateKey`, `draft` document and `draftTokens` are byte-identical before and after. This construction is the mirror image of `storefront-editor.test.ts` lines 57-62 and is what proves the gate is not trial-elevated rather than merely present.
2. **Forged key refusal.** Call `switchTemplate` with a string that is not in `TEMPLATE_KEYS`. Assert schema-level refusal and no write.
3. **Cross-tenant refusal.** Using tenant A's session, confirm no payload can cause a write to tenant B's rows. The schema carries no tenant field, so assert positively that tenant B's `storefront_theme` and `storefront_page` rows are untouched after tenant A's switch.
4. **Draft-only write (D-08).** A Professional merchant switches to a Professional template. Assert `draftTemplateKey`, `draft` and `draftTokens` all changed, and that `publishedTemplateKey`, `published` and `publishedTokens` are **byte-identical** to their pre-switch values. Compare the JSON columns by deep equality, not by truthiness.
5. **Accents and logo survive; copy tokens reset (D-11).** Before switching, set the tenant's `primaryAccent`, `secondaryAccent` and `logoKey` to distinctive values. After the switch, assert all three are unchanged, and assert `announcementText` and `footerTagline` equal the NEW template's defaults from `templateDefaultTokens`.
6. **Publish promotes all three.** After a switch, call `publishStorefront`. Assert `publishedTemplateKey` now equals the new key, and that `published` and `publishedTokens` equal the draft values — all three promoted in the one transaction.
7. **Discard reverts all three (D-09 / Pitfall 5).** After a switch and without publishing, call `discardDraft`. Assert `draftTemplateKey` is back to `publishedTemplateKey`, and that the reverted document equals the published one. Then repeat for a tenant that has **never published**, and assert the fallback document came from `templateDefaultDocument(publishedTemplateKey)` and not from the flagship — that is the specific regression Pitfall 5 names.

Do not use `$queryRaw` or `$executeRaw`; they are banned repository-wide. Read and write through `scopedDb(tenantId)` and the actions themselves.
